Accuracy and reaction speed test on moving targets.

Why it matters

Almost every sport involving a ball or equipment — basketball, tennis, boxing — relies on the connection between sight and hand movement. Faster, more precise coordination means fewer missed balls, quicker reactions in play, and lower injury risk.

What it measures

The speed and accuracy of your reaction to a moving visual stimulus — how quickly you turn what you see into a precise hand movement.

Who it helps

Ball and combat sport players, children in motor development, and people in rehabilitation after an injury.

Rating scale

< 400 msElite coordination
400–599 msExcellent coordination
600–799 msAverage coordination
≥ 800 msBelow average coordination

For comparison: experienced boxers react with a punch in around 402–405 ms — a similar level of hand-eye coordination is required in most ball sports.
