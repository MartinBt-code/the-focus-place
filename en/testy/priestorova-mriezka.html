Memorize the lit cells and click the same ones — order doesn't matter.

Why it matters

Unlike memorizing a sequence of steps, this is about capturing an entire visual pattern at once — exactly how a player has to memorize the opponent's formation on the field in a split second, or the layout of obstacles on a track.

What it measures

Visuospatial memory capacity for simultaneously presented patterns — how many cells you can memorize at once, regardless of order.

Who it helps

Athletes who need to quickly "snapshot" a formation on the field, and anyone wanting to train visual memory.

Rating scale

≥ 8 cellsExceptional spatial memory
6–7 cellsExcellent spatial memory
4–5 cellsAverage spatial memory
< 4 cellsBelow average spatial memory

The test is inspired by the Visual Patterns Test (Della Sala et al., 1997), which measures visuospatial memory capacity for simultaneously presented patterns — unlike sequential tests (e.g. the Corsi test), order doesn't matter here, only whether you remember the correct cells. The average adult can memorize around 4–5 cells at once, trained individuals reach 8 or more.
