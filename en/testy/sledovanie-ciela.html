Remaining

Keep your mouse cursor on the moving target for as long as possible over 20 seconds.

Why it matters

Tracking a moving object with your eyes while simultaneously "chasing" it with your hand is a fundamental skill in almost every ball sport — from following the ball in flight to keeping visual contact with an opponent changing direction.

What it measures

Smooth pursuit of a moving target combined with motor control — the ability to keep the cursor on a target that changes direction and speed.

Who it helps

Athletes who need to track a fast-moving object with both their eyes and body, and anyone wanting to train coordination and focus.

Rating scale

≥ 85%Exceptional tracking
70–84%Excellent tracking
50–69%Average tracking
< 50%Below average tracking

Smooth pursuit is one of the fundamental types of eye movement studied in vision science, and it's commonly trained as part of sports vision programs. Tracking accuracy drops as the target's speed and unpredictability increase — this test combines both.
