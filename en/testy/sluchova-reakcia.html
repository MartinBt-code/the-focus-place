The rating is based on the average of all attempts — for a more objective result we recommend at least 5 repetitions. Turn your sound on.

Why it matters

Not every important cue in sport is visual — a referee's whistle, a teammate's call, the sound of a start signal. Auditory reaction is usually faster than visual, because a sound signal travels to the brain along a shorter neural pathway. This test lets you compare both reaction channels.

What it measures

How fast you react to a simple sound cue — the time between the sound signal and your click, measured in milliseconds.

Who it helps

Athletes reacting to sound signals (start, whistle, calls), and anyone wanting to compare their visual and auditory reaction.

Auditory reaction is on average 30–50 ms faster than visual — a sound signal travels from the ear to the motor cortex along a shorter neural pathway than an image from the retina. A healthy adult typically averages around 140–160 ms.
