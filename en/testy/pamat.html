Memorize and repeat a growing sequence of tiles. How many levels can you reach?

Why it matters

Working memory lets you briefly hold and process information — game signals, tactical instructions, sequences of steps. The better you train it, the faster you learn new combinations and the fewer mistakes you make under pressure.

What it measures

The capacity and accuracy of short-term memory — how many steps in a sequence you can remember and repeat in the correct order.

Who it helps

Athletes learning game systems, coaches assessing cognitive readiness, and anyone who wants to train their memory.

Rating scale

≥ 10 levelsExceptional memory
7–9 levelsExcellent memory
4–6 levelsAverage memory
< 4 levelsBelow average memory

For comparison: on similar spatial memory tests (e.g. the Corsi block-tapping test), the average adult reaches around 5–6 levels — results below 4 or above 8 are rare in the general population.
