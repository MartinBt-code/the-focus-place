Study a list of 12 words, then type as many as you can remember. Order doesn't matter.

Why it matters

Free recall (without a hint) is harder than recognition — you have to pull the information out of memory yourself, not just confirm you know it. This is exactly what happens when an athlete has to recall a series of instructions from a coach without anyone reminding them.

What it measures

Declarative memory capacity for freely recalled information — how many words from a list you can retrieve without a cue.

Who it helps

Anyone who needs to remember a list of instructions, names, or notes without writing them down, and athletes learning instructions.

Rating scale

≥ 10 / 12Exceptional memory
7–9 / 12Excellent memory
4–6 / 12Average memory
< 4 / 12Below average memory

The test is based on the classic free recall paradigm, commonly used in memory research since Murdock's work (1962). On a 10–15 word list, a healthy adult typically recalls 5–9 items — words at the start and end of the list are remembered best (the primacy and recency effects).
